## Getting Legally Married in France as Foreign Nationals

### Civil Ceremony (Mariage Civil)
A legal marriage in France must take place at a French mairie (town hall). The civil ceremony is the legally binding event; a religious or symbolic ceremony can follow.

### Key Requirements
- At least one of the two partners must have their principal residence in France, OR have resided in France for at least 40 days immediately prior to the publication of banns
- Alternatively, couples can marry in the mairie of a commune where a parent of either partner resides

### Timeline
- **Publication of banns:** Must be displayed at the mairie for a minimum of 10 days before the ceremony can take place
- **Administrative file submission:** Typically 2–3 months before the desired date (some mairies require more)
- **Start the process:** We recommend beginning at least 6 months before the wedding for non-residents

### Required Documents (typical list — varies by nationality)
- Valid passports or national ID cards
- Full birth certificates (translated into French by a certified translator if not originally in French)
- Proof of address
- Certificate of celibacy or non-opposition to marriage (from your home country authorities)
- If previously divorced: decree absolute or divorce certificate (translated)
- Names and addresses of two witnesses per partner (total 4 witnesses, or 2 shared witnesses)

### Symbolic Ceremonies
Many of our international clients opt for a symbolic ceremony only — no legal paperwork in France, married legally in their home country before or after. This removes most of the administrative burden and allows full creative freedom for the ceremony itself.
